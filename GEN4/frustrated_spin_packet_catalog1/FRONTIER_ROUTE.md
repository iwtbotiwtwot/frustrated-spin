# Applying the packet method at frontier sizes

The exact composition method is not mathematically limited to N120. Larger explicitly defined packet-family sources can use the same cached local polynomials, repeated-packet powers, and exact boundary messages. Their total output and integer coefficients grow with N; runtime is therefore not constant. The present campaign measures only N1–120, and its execution guard preserves N121–144 as unexecuted holdouts.

There are two separate applications:

1. Extend the demonstrated packet families to larger N while preserving small packet boundaries. The existing exact identities apply whenever their explicit source conditions hold.
2. Apply the same compiler principle to the original connected frustrated frontier family by finding a decomposition of its actual interactions. A boundary of b spins requires up to 2^b conditional assignments. Small interfaces, repeated factors, exact symmetries and efficient contraction order are the features to seek.

A source-preserving structural scan of the already completed canonical N120 finds 300 interactions, no bridge edges, and no articulation vertices. Removing its retained eleven-spin cutset leaves one connected 109-spin graph. Its existing plan has 2,048 branches and retained width 21. Thus the one-gateway chain reduction cannot be directly substituted for its retained exact computation. Multiple-spin interfaces remain a concrete next direction.

The next computational study should use completed canonical N120 as the development target: identify small separators or reusable conditional subgraphs; compile exact boundary-conditioned packet messages; compare the full result with its retained authority; measure fresh and reusable work separately. This tests the transfer without consuming untouched frontier sources.

No N121–144 source was generated or executed in this study. Training remains paused.
