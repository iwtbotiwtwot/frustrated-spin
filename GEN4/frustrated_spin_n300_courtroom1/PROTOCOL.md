# N300 packet-family courtroom test — frozen protocol

Owner/originator/conceptual director: Sean Brady. AI research collaborators: OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex.

Owner request: a courtroom-style N300 test with precommitment, wrong controls, provenance, and a prediction of completion in less than 25 ms based on the recent packet-family findings. This is a new additive campaign; prior results and the paused training campaign remain unchanged.

## Prediction and primary endpoint

The primary prospective prediction is that the original packet-family N300 source completes a fresh global exact solve in **strictly less than 25 ms median over 31 prespecified warm-cache repetitions** on this workstation's Intel Core i9-12900HK CPU. Signed-packet N300 and connected-signed-packet N300 have separately prespecified predictions against the identical threshold. Every raw duration, the first solve, minimum, median, p95, maximum, and fraction below threshold will be reported. No outlier is discarded and no trial is repeated to improve the score.

This timing scope matches the recent catalog's warm metric: source-record binding, exact packet polynomial composition, construction of all scalar and ordered-port DOS coefficients, integer-to-string conversion inside the answer, canonical answer hash, configuration counts, and first/second moment checks. Structural compilation, loading previously verified local packet tables, independent full-answer comparison, DomainSession receipt/file writing, and process startup are outside that interval and recorded separately. No complete N300 output may be present in the input cache. Zero-glue open operators are derived and checked after the primary timer, as in the previous catalog.

The under-25-ms claim is a warm exact-calculation claim, not a process-launch, independent-certification, or file-persistence claim. Five empty-cache solves per family are secondary measurements with the same solve scope. One uninterrupted process performs all measurements; CPU affinity and the existing resource-manager budget are retained.

## Frozen sources

Build N300 directly from the three explicit N120 packet-family sources. Preserve all first-120 fields/interactions. Add twelve identical 15-spin packets, each three K5 layers joined by perfect matchings. The resulting disconnected sources have twenty 15-spin packets and 800 internal interactions. Fields remain zero. The signed source keeps one negative edge per K5 layer. The connected source continues the one-gateway bridge chain to 19 bridges and 819 interactions. Original ordered ports remain [0,1,60,61]. Signed complete graphs have 60 explicit local frustration triangles.

The three complete N300 source files, source/graph/port hashes, inherited N120 records, cache bytes, method code, runtime verification, hardware identity, and protocol are sealed before any N300 solve or timing. N121–144 sources are neither generated nor executed. A source definition containing 300 labeled vertices is not a sequence of intermediate-size experiments.

## Prediction basis

Prior pod N120 warm medians were 6.222 ms (packet), 6.393 ms (signed), and 6.848 ms (connected signed). Linear size scaling by 300/120 gives approximately 15.55, 15.98, and 17.12 ms. The predicted ceiling allows headroom for larger integer coefficients and output work. Earlier workstation N100/N105 fresh elimination was about 15–16% faster than the pod; this motivates the workstation trial but is not applied as a guaranteed hardware multiplier. These are prospective extrapolations, recorded before N300 execution.

## Execution order

1. Read and verify the frozen seal and independently retained precommit copy.
2. Qualify the copied algorithm against the retained N120 result for all three families; confirm complete equality. Qualification is not N300 training or timing.
3. Compile the three N300 source-bound plans and confirm every needed local packet table was already in the frozen N1–120 cache. Record preparation independently.
4. Perform exactly 31 timed warm solves per family. Round r uses the fixed family order cyclically shifted by r mod 3; all inputs and outputs are bound to family identity. The very first N300 solve is a scored observation. No target-size warmup is allowed.
5. Execute one independent fresh full-graph variable-elimination reference per family and compare every coefficient from every retained measurement hash. Write full reference and computed answers. Count and first/second moments are necessary checks, not substitutes for coefficient equality.
6. Perform five empty-cache solves per family. Compare each full answer with its independent reference. These do not affect the warm endpoint.
7. Execute eleven separately reported warm boundary-equality refinement repetitions on connected signed N300. These cannot replace the primary timings.
8. Run the wrong controls below and retain their exact altered inputs and rejection evidence.
9. Record all endpoint outcomes; checkpoint, verify final custody, and write the detailed report. The original prediction remains unchanged whatever the outcome.

## Wrong controls

- **Stale source/plan binding:** change one coupling but supply the original compiled plan. The bound solver must reject before using its local cache.
- **Undeclared cross-packet interaction:** add an edge not listed in the partition interface. Compilation must reject it.
- **Wrong-family cache:** replace the signed packet's local polynomials with positive-coupling tables under deliberately forged keys. Full-spectrum comparison or native exact checks must detect the wrong calculation. If count/moment checks alone accept it, document that and require full coefficient inequality.
- **Moment-preserving coefficient corruption:** alter four equally spaced bins by a third finite difference, preserving zeroth, first and second moments and nonnegative counts. Full coefficient equality must reject it despite those checks passing.
- **Wrong ordered-port rows:** exchange two distinct conditional spectra. Scalar DOS and global moments remain unchanged; ordered-port comparison must reject the substitution.
- **Invalid symmetry shortcut:** add a field to one non-root packet in the connected N300 source. Its two gateway spectra must differ; the equal-boundary shortcut must decline and the full two-state calculation must match independent full-graph elimination.

The wrong controls test binding, arithmetic completeness, observable order, and the reduction's precise preconditions. They do not change the three registered target graphs or enter timing statistics.

## Outcome rules and custody

For each family: exact verification and all relevant controls must pass before interpreting its timing result. A warm median below 25 ms meets that family's prediction; a median at or above 25 ms misses it. A wiring/execution failure is retained as such and is not relabeled as a mathematical result. A correction requires a separately sealed amendment and preserves the initial attempt; it cannot silently replace a scored run.

Use the existing workstation resource manager and one serial CPU coordinator. No GPU configuration, system-service installation, hosted subagent, old training restart, or global selector change is part of this test. SHA-256 manifests, source files, a chained event ledger, native DomainSession receipts and a separately copied precommit provide provenance. The witness copy is independently stored, not an external trusted timestamp authority.

The scientific interpretation uses the project's established classifications, with performance endpoint and exactness checks reported explicitly. All previously closed holdouts and canonical answers remain preserved.
