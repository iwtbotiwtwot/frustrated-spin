# Rights, copyright and control

Prepared 2026-09-07 for Sean Brady. This guide records the practical rights
steps for this release; it is not a copyright registration, signed assignment
or opinion establishing exclusive ownership of every line.

## What the copyright notice accomplishes

The release notice is **Copyright © 2026 Sean Brady. All rights reserved**,
subject to the research-only licence and independently licensed material.
Copyright arises automatically in eligible original expression once fixed.
The notice identifies the stated owner and permission position; registration
is a separate act. Copyright protects eligible code and prose, not mathematical
facts, ideas, methods or algorithms as such. See the U.S. Copyright Office's
[copyright explanation](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) and
[computer-program registration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-programs.html).

The Software's licence grants noncommercial research permission while reserving
commercial permissions. It is not a substitute for documenting the rights
the licensor actually holds.

## Who should own the rights

The originating workspace names Sean Brady in its copyright notice. The
stewardship declaration names SAM Research Project LC as the entity receiving
specified commercial value. Those are distinct statements. This release keeps
Sean Brady as the stated licensor; it does not fabricate an assignment to the LC,
the SAM Foundation, or the GitHub organization.

If the intended owner is an entity, record its exact legal name and have the
existing owner execute an assignment covering the identified works and rights.
If the entity is to license on the owner's behalf, document that authority
instead. A U.S. copyright ownership transfer generally requires a writing
signed by the owner or authorized agent. A GitHub commit, organization name or
README alone does not supply that transfer. See
[17 U.S.C. §204](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html#204).

## AI-assisted work

OpenAI's [individual terms, Content section](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)
and [Services Agreement §4.1](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)
state, as between OpenAI and the user/customer and to the extent permitted by
law, that the user/customer owns output and receives OpenAI's rights, if any,
in that output. The applicable account, agreement and creation dates should
be recorded. An organization account can make the customer an entity rather
than the individual operator. This release does not infer which agreement
governed every historical session.

That contractual allocation is different from copyrightability. The U.S.
Copyright Office says AI-assisted works can qualify where sufficient expressive
elements are human-authored, including qualifying human modifications and
creative arrangement; prompts alone do not establish authorship of the output.
Preserve Sean's authored text, implemented edits, expressive design decisions
and selection/arrangement evidence. Do not classify all code as exclusively
human-written, or assert that conceptual direction alone establishes copyright
in every generated line. See the
[Copyright Office's January 2025 report announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html).

The project retains its research-collaboration attribution. That credit is not
an assertion of corporate partnership or a separate rights transfer.

## Human contributors and earlier licences

Before integrating a human contribution, identify the contributor's rights,
including any employer, client or institutional interest. Obtain a signed
assignment or a sufficient contributor licence. If Sean is to retain the option
of granting commercial permissions later, the incoming grant must cover
commercial sublicensing/relicensing as well as the current research licence.
A Developer Certificate of Origin alone need not grant that additional power.
The [contribution agreement template](CONTRIBUTOR_PERMISSION_TEMPLATE.md)
records the intended grant and must actually be completed and signed.

Earlier SLC public releases are recorded in project authority as AGPL-3.0-or-later
for code and CC BY-SA 4.0 for documents/data. A rights holder may offer their
own eligible work under another licence, but an earlier valid grant is not
withdrawn from its recipients. Third-party contributions cannot be relicensed
merely because they are in the same repository. See
[GNU's explanation of multiple licences](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#ReleaseUnderGPLAndNF).

For each included component, the provenance manifest records origin and exact
bytes. The local inspection found no separate copyright/licence headers in
the exported project code or its sealed V6 source archive. This observation
is not a substitute for ownership documents. The rights register explicitly
distinguishes project records from legal instruments supplied for review.

## Registration and stewardship implementation

For a U.S. registration, identify the correct author and claimant, publication
facts, eligible human-authored expression, excluded pre-existing/AI-generated
material as applicable, and the appropriate source-code deposit. Have an IP
lawyer review those facts and the custom licence before relying on them for
enforcement. Registration may strengthen enforcement options; it does not
convert unprotectable material into copyright. See
[Circular 61](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ61.pdf) and the
[registration portal](https://www.copyright.gov/registration/).

For any later commercial agreement, expressly implement the existing 90%
stewardship floor on the defined receipts of SAM Research Project LC or its
successor. Name the beneficiary, allowable costs, accounting and audit process,
enforcement rights and successor obligations. The preserved declaration supplies
intent and provenance; the signed agreement and entity governance implement it.

## Owner records to assemble

1. The selected legal owner/licensor and any signed transfer or agency agreement.
2. Human-authorship evidence tied to files and commits; applicable AI-account terms.
3. Signed contributor/employer permissions for any additional rights holders.
4. Dependency notices and any earlier licences or third-party grants.
5. Any registration certificate and its accurate deposit/application record.
6. Executed stewardship governance and commercial agreements, when created.

Keep private signatures, addresses and account documents in the owner's legal
records. The repository can publish document IDs/status and appropriate public
notices without exposing those private records.
